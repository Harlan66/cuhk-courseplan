Study Scheme


Learning Outcomes
 

	

1.          Major Programme:

With its interdisciplinary approach, the Urban Studies Programme aims to overcome the compartmentalised thinking in separate fields. Students are expected to develop a capacity to integrate knowledge, skills, values and attitudes in comprehending and solving urban issues.

 

Graduates will have acquired the ability to:

 

Knowledge

(1)   explain, analyse and evaluate multi-scalar and multi-dimensional factors creating and influencing urban spaces and places over time that shape and affect people’s needs at different geographical levels;

(2)   appraise the roles of the governments, the market and the civil society at different geographical scales and over time in shaping the courses of urbanisation or urban transformation and to reflect on their impacts on the well-being of people, place and planet;

(3)   compare and contrast different modes of producing, consuming and distributing resources and generalise their impacts on the ecological landscape and the consequent well-being of individual and collective actors;

(4)   distinguish and reflect on various patterns of integration of cities in regional and global networks; and

(5)    identify specifics of urban development in Hong Kong and mainland China and develop an ability to create and design a sustainable future for the region.

 

Skills

(1)   apply qualitative and quantitative methods to analyse and differentiate the quality of spaces and places;

(2)   develop independent thinking, collaborative learning, communication and presentation skills and to apply them in team work setting to nurture co-learning and co-creating capacity; and

(3)   use visualisation techniques, spatial thinking and design and computer skills (GIS, remote sensing, environmental simulation, CAD, etc.) to comprehend, co-develop creative and inter-disciplinary solutions to resolve multi-dimensional urban problems.

 

Values and Attitudes

(1)   value collaboration, interdependence, empathy and integrity to build an inclusive urbanism that respects natural ecology and people’s dignity;

(2)   apply their global outlook, social-ecological awareness and professional ethics in their service to society;

(3)   appreciate the importance of preserving the character of place and its cultural value; and

(4)   develop a capacity to respect the diversity of human life and the environment through life-long learning.

 

2.          Minor Programme:

Upon the completion of the Minor Programme, students will have demonstrated basic understanding of fundamental issues related to urban studies such as various factors influencing multi-scalar and multi-dimensional urban development, planning and governance, acquired knowledge of some aspects of the subject with sufficient exposure and at a suitable level of skill competency to allow further study in this subject or related subjects or to pursue a related career.

 

While most skills and attitudes expected of a graduate would have been developed through the Major Programme, graduates who have been awarded a Minor will have developed the skills and attitudes that are related specifically to this Minor Programme.
