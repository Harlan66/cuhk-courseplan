Major Programme Requirement

 




Students are required to complete a minimum of 90 units of courses. Students will take the Aerospace Science and Earth Informatics offered by CUHK as the 1st major, and choose from one of the Majors offered by CUHK or CUHK(SZ) as specified in 3. below as their 2nd Major. Some courses offered by CUHK and CUHK(SZ) are substitute/equivalent/alternative courses. Please refer to the list of equivalent/substitute courses for details.

Explanatory Notes:




1.

	

Students enrolled in the Double Major Programme should normally spend four regular terms at CUHK(SZ) during the four years of study, except otherwise specified in 2. and 3. Below. It applies to all students, including those who select a 2nd Major offered by CUHK.




2.

	

Students may apply for the Co-operative Education (Co-op) Programme (5 units) in Year 2 or above. Students joining the Co-op Programme will undertake a full-time placement in two specific work terms, including one summer session and one regular term (i.e., term 1 or term 2). They have to spend at least three regular terms at CUHK(SZ) during the four years of study.




3.

	

Students who join term-time exchange programme have to spend at least three regular terms at CUHK(SZ) during the four years of study.




4.

	

Students enrolled in the Double Major programme can take substitute/equivalent/alternative courses either at CUHK or CUHK(SZ). The list of substitute/equivalent/alternative courses is available at this link (https://admission.cuhk.edu.hk/aseidm/). Students should not take a course and its substitute/equivalent/alternative course. Should they do so, only one course will be counted towards the fulfilment of units requirements. Grades of relevant substitute/equivalent/alternative courses taken at CUHK(SZ) will be transferred to CUHK for the purpose of calculation of GPA, Major GPA and credit units.




5.

	

Students normally have to declare their 2nd Major by the end of their first year of study.




6.

	

All courses at 2000 and above level and courses labeled as # in the requirements of the 1st Major Programme, as well as 3 units of research component course offered by the 2nd Major, will be included in the calculation of Major GPA for honours classification.




7.

	

Courses taken to fulfil the requirements of the 1st Major cannot be counted towards the fulfilment of requirements of the 2nd Major, and vice versa.




8.

	

Under very special circumstances and subject to approval by the Major programmes concerned, students may opt out of the Double Major Programme and register for the 1st Major programme only, and they will be required to take an additional 3 units of research component course offered by the 2nd Major to fulfill the unit requirement of the 1st Major which is 60 units. The 3 units of research component will also be included in the calculation of Major GPA. The decision to opt out is irrevocable. Students who have been approved to opt out from the Double Major Programme should still observe the requirements in 1. above. If the students have not yet declared a 2nd Major at the time of opting out, they should fulfil the requirements of one of the areas under the 1st Major and take the additional 3 units of research component offered by the 2nd Major corresponding to the particular area.




9.

	

Students may be required to complete prerequisite courses for certain courses listed in the study scheme.




[a]

	

Students taking Physics offered by CUHK as 2nd Major should are recommended to take MATH1010.




[b]

	

Students taking Physics offered by CUHK as 2nd Major should take PHYS1111.




[c]

	

For students taking Earth and Environmental Sciences as 2nd Major, research component EESC4820 will be included in the calculation of Major GPA for honours classification.

[a]

 

	

Students may apply to study in the Co-operative Education Programme (Co-op@CUHK) (5 units) in Year 2 or above. Students who study the Co-op programme will take COOP4001, which is a 1-unit course to be taken in each of the Summer Session of Year 3 and Term 1 of Year 4. They will undertake a full-time placement in two specific work terms, during which they are not allowed to register for any other courses except the 1-unit continuous assessment course COOP4001. For students who have to take more than 1 unit of course in the work term, they should provide strong justifications for each additional course for approval by the Co-operative Education Programme Committee and the major programme.

	


[b]

	

When students are studying at CUHK(SZ), they can take substitute/equivalent/alternative courses at CUHK(SZ) to fulfill the requirements of CUHK.

	


[c]

	

The recommended course pattern of College GE courses varies according to student’s college affiliation.

	


[d]

	

Approval has been granted by the Registrar for students of the Aerospace Science and Earth Informatics & X Double Major Programme to take 19 units of courses in Term 1 and/or Term 2 of Year 1.

	


[e]

	

Students have to take 3 units of courses from item 2(a)(ii) of the study scheme. These courses may be offered in other terms.

	


[f]

	

If students have taken a 3-unit UGE course at CUHK(SZ), the 3 units will be transferred to CUHK. Currently all GE courses in CUHK(SZ) are 3-unit.

[a]

 

	

Students may apply to study in the Co-operative Education Programme (Co-op@CUHK) (5 units) in Year 2 or above. Students who study the Co-op programme will take COOP4001, which is a 1-unit course to be taken in each of the Summer Session of Year 3 and Term 1 of Year 4. They will undertake a full-time placement in two specific work terms, during which they are not allowed to register for any other courses except the 1-unit continuous assessment course COOP4001. For students who have to take more than 1 unit of course in the work term, they should provide strong justifications for each additional course for approval by the Co-operative Education Programme Committee and the major programme.

Study Scheme


Learning Outcomes
 

	

1. Major Programme:

 

Upon completion of the Programme, students will have:



Knowledge outcomes

Interdisciplinary knowledge

By completing the programme, graduates will have acquired knowledge of:

(1)      various theories, concepts and methods of aerospace science and Earth system, mechanical engineering and automation, energy and environment, geography and earth information science, and their applications;

(2)      the Earth and its environment, including their inter-dependent nature for developing an in-depth understanding of their relationships with space exploration and navigation; and

(3)      aerospace industry in the Greater Bay Area and beyond.

Analyzing/Application Ability

By completing the programme, graduates will have developed the following abilities:

(1)   a life-long ability to analyze, explain and reflect on real-world earth information, spatial development and resource management issues covered in the curriculum; and

(2)   a capacity to apply various theories and methods of earth information science and resource management in their further studies and career development.

 

Skills Outcomes

Generic Competencies

By completing the programme, graduates will demonstrate competency in:

(1)    integrating knowledge in science and engineering, and solving interdisciplinary problems through quantitative and qualitative data analysis;

(2)      effective communication, management, teamwork and leadership;

(3)      ethical values and responsibilities as professional member of local and world communities; and

(4)      motivation in professional development, and self-managed and life-long learning.

Professional Skills

By completing the programme, graduates will have acquired professional skills on:

(1)    identifying key issues related to aerospace science and the Earth system;

(2)      applying knowledge of engineering sciences and mathematics to the discipline of aerospace science, earth informatics, mechanical and automation engineering, energy technologies and environments;

(3)      designing and conducting experiments, as well as analyzing and interpreting data; communicating and conveying results of the analysis to both the general public and professional practitioners;

(4)      generating and evaluating alternatives, developing practical solutions and proposing strategies in a global environment; and

(5)      problem-solving in an inter-disciplinary context.

 

Attitude/Value Outcomes

By completing the programme, graduates will have acquired a positive attitude on:

(1)    awareness of the importance of engaging in life-long learning and staying abreast of contemporary issues;

(2)      appreciation of different aspects of knowledge in aerospace science and technology;

(3)      the continuing development of aerospace fields and the Earth system;

(4)      embracing the perspective of sustainable development and becoming sensitive to their responsibilities with reference to spatial development and resource management issues; and

(5)      developing a capacity to compare, contrast and respect the social, economic and environmental values of places at different geographical scales and the significance of local and regional conditions in the context of globalization.
