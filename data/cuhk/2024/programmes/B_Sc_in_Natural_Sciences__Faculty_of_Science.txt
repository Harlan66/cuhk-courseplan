Major Programme Requirement (for Associate Degree or Higher Diploma holders admitted to senior-year places)

 




Students are required to complete a minimum of 51 units of courses as follows:




 




Students must choose one concentration area and take at least 32 units of courses (including at least 3 units of research course(s)) among the subject areas prescribed under required courses and corresponding concentration area courses, namely Biological Sciences, Biotechnology, Environmental Studies, Food and Nutrition, Data Science, Chemical and Testing Sciences, and Physical Sciences.  Students will fulfill this unit requirement automatically by taking the relevant courses as prescribed in each concentration area.  Also, students shall take no more than 11 units of MATH courses at 3000-5000 level, no more than 10 units from the following CMBI courses: CMBI4001, 4002, 4003, 4101, 4102, 4103, 4201, 4202, 4203, 4301, 4302, 4303, and no more than 15 units from the following PHYS courses: PHYS1001, 1002, 1111, 1122, 2041, 2051, 2401, 3011, 3021, 3022, 3031, 3041, 3051, 3061, 3420, 3730, 4011, 4021, 4031, 4041, 4050, 4051, 4420, 4430, 4440, 4450, 4460, 4470, for the purpose of fulfilling the requirement of this programme. Upon completion of all courses prescribed under a concentration area, the concentration area will be printed on academic transcript.

Study Scheme


Learning Outcomes
 

	

Major Programme:  




Graduates will have acquired broad knowledge in science and in-depth knowledge across several science subjects in a concentration are 4 mastered a basket of professional and generic skills for career development, and possessed attitudes and values with a view to contributing to the advancement and betterment of the society.




 

	

 




Knowledge




Graduates will have developed a set of knowledge outcomes with variations according to the concentration areas that they have chosen. They will have acquired




(1)

	

basic knowledge in science; and




(2)

	

an in-depth understanding in at least one concentration area.




 

	

 




Skills




Professional skills




Graduates will have acquired a good mastery of professional skills, including




(1)

	

capability to use scientific methods for problem solving and decision making;




(2)

	

skills to use quantitative and statistical methods to process and analyze data;




(3)

	

ability to conduct independent research, including literature search, collection and analysis of evidence, and writing scientific reports; and;




(4)

	

effective presentation and writing skills to communicate scientific findings to lay persons.




 

	

 




Generic skills




Graduates will have mastered a basket of generic skills, including  




(1)

	

competence in using information technologies;




(2)

	

ability to work independently and in a team;




(3)

	

ability to make judgments about alternative perspectives and different viewpoints;




(4)

	

ability to explore and propose new ideas;




(5)

	

effective presentation and writing skills to communicate viewpoints; and




(6)

	

capability of self-learning.




 

	

 




Values and attitudes




Graduates will have developed positive values and attitudes, including




(1)

	

appreciation of science;




(2)

	

awareness on the latest development of science;




(3)

	

appreciation and advocacy of thinking with logical rigour;




(4)

	

responsible attitudes to judge the pros and cons of science applications on human society and  natural environment; and




(5)

	

motivation for life-long learning.
