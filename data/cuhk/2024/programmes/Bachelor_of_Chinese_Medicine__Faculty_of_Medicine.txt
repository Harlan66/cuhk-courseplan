Major Programme Requirement

 

The Programme consists of two parts: the Pre-clinical Programme (4.5 years) and the Clinical Programme (1.5 years).

 

Pre-clinical Programme

In the Pre-clinical Programme (4.5 years), students have to complete both required and elective courses including subjects in basic medical and life sciences, Chinese medical concepts and principles of Chinese medicine application, Chinese medicine classics and history, specialties in Chinese medicine and clinical training.  Students who take “Clinical Training” courses (i.e. BCME 2401-2405) may need to undergo training in summer vacation.

 

Clinical Programme

The Clinical Programme extends over 1.5 years. The first year provides students with teaching in clinical settings of clinics and wards in a Chinese medicine hospital in the Mainland. Clinical training will cover various specialties in Chinese medicine including internal medicine, acupuncture and bone setting. Students will rotate among attachments to various clinical departments and write reports on case histories of selected topics. During the last six months, students will receive advanced clinical training in one or two selected specialties by attaching to a Chinese medicine hospital in the Mainland and/ or a specialty clinic in Hong Kong. They will also learn about the operation of Chinese medicine clinics in Hong Kong and the diagnosis and treatment of common medical problems seen in out-patient clinic settings. A professional examination will be arranged for students at the end of each term to assess their academic performance.  Students may need to undergo training during summer and winter vacations.

Study Scheme


Learning Outcomes
 

	

 

Major Programme:

The key expected learning outcomes (i.e. knowledge, skills, and values/attitude, for graduates of the Bachelor of Chinese Medicine programme) are as follows:

 

Knowledge Outcomes

Graduates will have possessed broad and in-depth knowledge related to Chinese Medicine, including the following:

(1)   basic concepts and historical development of Chinese medicine;

(2)   basic principles underpinning the use of Chinese materia medica and herbal formulary;

(3)   basic principles underpinning the practice of acupuncture and moxibustion;

(4)   etiology, pathomechanisms of common diseases in clinical specialties of Chinese medicine;

(5)   Chinese medicine classics;

(6)   fundamental concepts in life sciences;

(7)   basic principles of diagnostics and therapeutics in Western medicine; and

(8)   basic concepts of medical statistics.

 

Skills Outcomes

Generic competencies

Graduates should acquire generic competences, including the following:

(1)   communication and interpersonal skills;

(2)   presentation skills for succinctly and clearly communicating medical and scientific findings to an audience of professionals or lay persons;

(3)   competence in utilizing computer and the internet to search for information; and

(4)   analytical skills and critical thinking for problem solving.

 

Professional skills

Graduates should have full competence in professional skills, including the following:

(1)   diagnostic methods and pattern differentiation in Chinese medicine;

(2)   formulating treatment strategies based on disease diagnosis and pattern differentiation;

(3)   applying common Chinese herbs and formulas for treatment of diseases;

(4)   locating common acupuncture points and practicing acupuncture and moxibustion techniques in clinical setting;

(5)   mastering tuina manual techniques and their application in treatment;

(6)   ability to manage a Chinese medicine clinic as a business; and

(7)   information management skills such as inputting and retrieval of medical data.

 

Attitude/Value outcomes

Graduates should have a positive attitude towards issues concerning Chinese Medicine practice and development, including the following:

(1)   conforming to professional ethics and codes of conduct;

(2)   an awareness of the latest developments and advancements in Chinese medicine;

(3)   recognizing the strengths and limitations of Chinese and Western medicine;

(4)   recognizing the need for further personal and professional development; and

(5)   ability in adapting to the changing social and health-care environment so as to stay competitive.
