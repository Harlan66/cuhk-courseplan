Explanatory Notes:




1.

	

ECON courses at 2000 and above level will be included in the calculation of Major GPA for honours classification.




[a]

	

Students who have obtained grade 3 or above in both the compulsory part and Module 1 (Calculus and Statistics) of the HKDSE Mathematics examination may, upon application, be exempted from ECON1101, in which case the Major Programme Requirement will be reduced by 2 units. 

Students who have obtained grade 3 or above in both the compulsory part and Module 2 (Algebra and Calculus) of the HKDSE Mathematics examination are not required to take ECON1101. The Major Programme Requirement for these students will be reduced by 2 units.

Students who have obtained (i) grade B or above in GCE Mathematics (AL) and grade B or above in GCE Further Mathematics (AL) or (ii) grade B or above in GCE Mathematics (AL) and grade B or above in GCE Pure Mathematics (AL) are not required to take ECON1101. The Major Programme Requirement for these students will be reduced by 2 units. 




[b]

	

Students are allowed to take ECON courses at 5000 or above level to fulfill the Elective Courses requirement, subject to the consent of the course instructor.




[c]

	

Students may declare a concentration area (i) after the requisite courses for the concentration area have been completed, or (ii) in the same term that the requisite courses are expected to be completed. Upon completion of all requisite courses for a concentration area, such concentration area will be indicated on academic transcripts.




[d]

	

Students are required to declare which MATH course is to be counted towards the requirement of the Economic Theory concentration area at their final term of attendance.

Study Scheme


Learning Outcomes
 

	

 

1.          Major Programme:

Graduates of this programme will achieve the following Knowledge, Skills, and Values and Attitudes Outcomes. 

 

Knowledge Outcomes

Knowledge of basic economic theory and real-world economic issues including: 

(1)   Using powerful analytical tools to interpret complex economic phenomena;

(2)   issues related to Greater-China and the world economy in an age of globalization, with an appreciation of the power and limitations of theory;

 

Skills Outcomes

Competence in basic statistics, econometrics, and mathematics, including:

(1)   the ability to apply empirical and mathematical tools in testing economic theory and analyzing real-world economic issues, and to make informed judgments on controversial economic issues and policy debates; and

(2)   the ability to understand and predict emerging economic trends in an age of globalization so as to facilitate career planning and the management of  personal, corporate, and public finance in response to challenges from a fast-changing world.

 

Competence in a set of generic skills essential to the modern age, including:

(1)   the ability to articulate ideas in verbal and written form;

(2)   bilingual proficiency in meeting the challenges of globalization and Hong Kong as a gateway to China;

(3)   logical and critical thinking as a foundation for life-long learning and future professional development; and

(4)   interpersonal communications and collaboration skills.

 

Values and Attitudes Outcomes

A set of personal attributes conducive to good work and personal ethics for the 21st century, including:

(1)   honesty and integrity, with emphasis on respecting intellectual property rights; 

(2)   cross-cultural sensitivity and tolerance as personal attributes needed to thrive in an increasingly internationalized workforce;

(3)   the ability to engage in rational discussions in an increasingly transparent and participatory society; and

(4)   embracing the philosophy of life-long learning.

 

2.          Minor Programme:

Upon the completion of the Minor Programme in Economics, students will have demonstrated basic understanding of fundamental issues related to economics such as:

l   fundamental theories that explain market behaviour and changes in economic variables at the micro and macro levels,

l   the scientific approach of testing theories using empirical data, and

l   the sensitivity to emerging economic trends that affect career planning and the management of personal and corporate finance in a fast-changing world.

 

Students will have acquired knowledge of basic aspects of economics with sufficient exposure and at a suitable level to allow further study in economics-related subjects or to pursue a related career.
