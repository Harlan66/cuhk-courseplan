Explanatory Notes:




1.

	

PHAR courses at 2000 and above level as well as those labeled as # will be included in the calculation of Major GPA for honours classification.




2.

	

Students are encouraged to take 1–5 units of free elective courses from PHAR2018, 3603, 3604 and 3605 in the summer session to gain experience in pharmacy practice.  However, these free elective courses shall not be counted towards the calculation of Major GPA for honours classification.




3.

	

Students must obtain Grade "C+" or above in the following courses, including PHAR3603, 3604, 3605, 3812, 4201, 4204, 4205, 4301, 4302, 4303, 4304, 4305, 4306, 4307, 4308, 4309, 4310, 4350, 4401, 4402, 4403, 4501, 4601, 4901 and 4912, in order to fulfill Major Programme Requirement. Those who obtain Grade "C" or below may repeat the same course or take a substitute course to fulfill the Major Programme Requirement in the subsequent year.




4.

	

Students are required to declare the Area of Concentration in June of the year immediately before their final year of attendance.




[a]

	

Students obtained a total score of at least 38 in 6 HKDSE subjects, including at least 5* in English, Biology and Chemistry, are eligible to apply for course and unit exemptions for MEDF1011 and MEDF1012, to be granted by the Board of the Faculty of Medicine on a case-by-case basis on behalf of the Senate.  Students who have been granted such exemptions are still required to fulfill the overall unit requirement of the Major Programme.




[b]

	

Only a number of Pharmacy Clerkship courses will be offered in each academic year in accordance with resources available in that particular academic year.




[c]

	

Students are encouraged to take two major elective courses. If students take more than 1 Major Elective Course, the surplus course(s) shall also be counted towards the calculation of Major GPA for honours classification.

Study Scheme


Learning Outcomes
 

	

1.         Major Programme:

Upon the completion of the Pharmacy programme, the graduate is:

 

(1)  Professional knowledge and application (K/S)

-         prepared with scientific and professional knowledge and skills for contemporary pharmaceutical practice;

-         equipped with qualitative and quantitative/statistical analysis skills, and be able to collect, interpret and manage scientific and clinical data;

-         able to adopt a systematic approach in analyzing and solving problems in the manufacture and quality assurance of pharmaceutical products;

 

(2)  Professional attitude and conduct (S/V)

-         able to accept responsibility for his/her own actions and decisions;

-         able to apply relevant ethical and legal principles in the practice of pharmacy;

-         able to uphold the professional code of conduct;

 

(3)  Professional communication (S)

-         able to communicate effectively with patients, peers, other health professionals and the public;

-         confident and competent in counselling patients and clients on prescription, non-prescription drugs and consumer health products;

 

(4)  Information evaluation and decision-making (K/S)

-         able to retrieve and critically evaluate medical/pharmaceutical literature;

-         able to identify and appropriately utilize drug information resources to assist with patient-specific drug therapy monitoring;

-         confident in making decisions on patients’ drug therapy and recommending appropriate treatment; and

 

(5)  Continuing education and lifelong learning (K/S/V)

-         able to build upon his/her knowledge and skills and engage in lifelong continuing education and professional development.

 

K = Knowledge outcomes S = Skills outcomes V = Values and attitudes outcomes
